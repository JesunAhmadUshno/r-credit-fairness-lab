R Credit Fairness Lab: Rebuilding a Credit-Risk Champion in R and Auditing Its Group Fairness

1 Research question

Does an R-native rebuild of the credit-risk champion from the Python lab credit-risk-lab (tidymodels: regularized logistic regression, gradient boosting through the xgboost engine, plus a GAM arm) reproduce the Python champion’s discrimination on the same UCI Taiwan credit data and the same locked split, and where do group fairness gaps (equal opportunity, predictive parity) sit across sex, age bands, and education?

The study was pre-registered in PREREGISTRATION.md, committed before any model was fit. Hypotheses, split, threshold, and verdict rules were frozen there.

2 Data and the locked split

Data: UCI dataset 350, “default of credit card clients” (Taiwan, 2005), 30,000 accounts, committed byte-identical in this repo (SHA-256 checked by the pipeline). Loading, recodes, and feature engineering mirror credit-risk-lab line for line.

The split is the Python lab’s split: stratified 70/30, train_test_split(random_state = 42). It is replicated in pure R (R/sklearn_split.R, a numpy-compatible MT19937 plus the StratifiedShuffleSplit steps) and proven identical, not approximated:

3 Arms and headline results

Arm 1 is ridge logistic regression (glmnet via tidymodels, penalty tuned by 5-fold cross-validation on the training set only; chosen penalty 0.00010). Arm 2 is gradient boosting with the xgboost engine at the Python champion’s hyperparameters (300 trees, learning rate 0.06, depth 3). Arm 3 is a GAM fit directly with mgcv, with smooth terms for the continuous features: full interpretability is its purpose.

H1 asked whether the R GBM AUC lands within 0.01 of the Python champion’s 0.7810. H2 asked whether the GAM loses no more than 0.02 AUC against the GBM. H3 asked whether at least one protected attribute shows an equal-opportunity gap above 5 percentage points at the champion threshold.

The GAM’s smooth terms (effective degrees of freedom and significance) are in results/gam_terms.csv, with the full mgcv summary in results/gam_summary.txt.

5 Claims ceiling

This is a study on public Taiwan 2005 data. It evidences method only: reproducing a published pipeline in a second language and auditing its group fairness on the same split. It is not production experience, not a Canadian portfolio result, and not a claim about any real lender. The societal stake it speaks to is fair access to credit in Canada: a champion model whose errors fall unevenly on protected groups is a finding about the model, and it matters most exactly where credit decisions gate people’s lives.
